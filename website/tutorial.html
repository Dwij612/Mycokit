Other mushroom shapes

The five steps are for a mushroom with a stem and cap. For any other shape, spread the pins out from the base to the far end, a few centimetres apart, and push each one in once. The black reference pin always goes into what the mushroom grows from, never into the mushroom.

Bracket

Grows on wood, like turkey tail and reishi.

  • RInto the wood, a hand’s width from the bracket.
  • 12Near where it meets the wood: 1 from above, 2 from below.
  • 3Into the middle, from above.
  • 4Into the soft outer edge.

Tip: old brackets can be as hard as wood. If a pin won’t go in, don’t force it: move it towards the soft, pale edge.

Cluster

Grows in a bunch, like oyster mushrooms and maitake.

  • RInto the block or log, a hand’s width from the bunch.
  • 12Into the base where the caps join, one from each side.
  • 3Into the middle of the biggest cap.
  • 4Into the outer edge of the same cap.

Tip: pick one bunch and keep all four pins in it.

Ball

No stem or cap, like lion’s mane and puffballs.

  • RInto the block or soil, a hand’s width away.
  • 12Low down, near where it sits, one from each side.
  • 3Into the top.
  • 4Into the side, where the spines hang. No spines? Halfway down.

Tip: in a grow bag, push the pins through the plastic and keep the bag closed. Lion’s mane bruises easily, so don’t wiggle a pin once it’s in. Pick a firm puffball: old, papery ones burst into spores.

These are starting points, not rules. Nobody knows the best spot on each shape yet. When you share a recording, add a note saying which shape it was and where the pins went.

For a cleaner signal

  • Give it a couple of minutes. Right after the pins go in, the lines wander while things settle. MycoKit waits about 2 minutes before it suggests anything.
  • Hands off while it records. Touching or bumping the kit makes spikes that aren't the mushroom. Scientists even pause their recordings when they open the box.
  • Keep the wires slack. A wire that pulls on a pin moves it, and that shows up on the line.
  • Keep it damp. Mushrooms dry out fast. The scientists keep theirs in humid boxes.
  • Keep away from chargers and appliances. They give off electrical noise the probes can pick up.
  • Be patient. The signals are tiny and slow. A quiet line is a result too.

Words you'll see

Probe
One of the four thin pins that go into the mushroom. Each one gets its own line on the screen.
Reference pin
The black pin that goes in the soil. Every probe is measured against it. You may hear it called "ground" — it has nothing to do with the ground you walk on.
Settling
The first couple of minutes after the pins go in, when the lines wander before they steady.
Unusual activity
When a probe suddenly moves much more than it did over the last few minutes. MycoKit beeps and marks it on that probe's line.
Millivolt (mV)
A thousandth of a volt. Mushroom signals are smaller still.
Sonification
Turning the signal into sound. One minute of recording plays as one second.
Recording ID
A short code like FK-7Q2M that names one recording. You won't need to type it: it's inside the bundle, with your photos. (It isn't the access code, which is printed on the kit.)

Where this advice comes from

  • Pins in the stem and cap, a few centimetres apart: Adamatzky, On spiking behaviour of oyster fungi, Scientific Reports, 2018.
  • Pausing recordings when the box is opened, and keeping fungi humid: Detection of electrical signals in fungal mycelia in response to external stimuli, iScience, 2025.
  • How small the signals are, and why where you measure matters: Buffi and colleagues, Electrical signaling in fungi: past and present challenges, FEMS Microbiology Reviews, 2025.
  • Placement on brackets, clusters and balls: a starting point from the project team, following the same base-to-far-end idea. It has not been tested yet.
  • Cleaning the tips and the reference pin in the soil: the MycoKit project team.
